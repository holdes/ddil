## THE METHOD CHANGE THAT ENDED THE SERVO BRACKET SAGA

`servo_bracket` took **four revisions and nine faults**. Every fault came from
the same root cause: the bracket was checked against a **list of numbers** I had
transcribed, not against the part it holds. Faults found that way surface one at
a time, in whatever order the builder happens to hit them.

The fix, now standard for any part receiving a bought component:

1. **Model the component as a SOLID** — `cad/mg90s.py`, `cad/servo_horn.py`.
2. **Measure it from the vendor file in code.** Never transcribe. `mg90s.py`
   measures every dimension off `MG90S.3MF` at import.
3. **Boolean the part against it**, both SEATED and SWEPT along the whole
   insertion path. The sweep is what catches "fits once in place, can't get
   there".
4. **Assert what must NOT fit too** — the bracket rejects an upside-down servo
   by 875 mm³, which turned a bench surprise into a build-guide line.

This found, within minutes of existing: a shaft placed 5.42 mm wrong, a flip
sign wrong twice, a hub the horn never entered, and a sweep running backwards.

**Vendor photo callouts are not drawings.** The MG90S marketing image disagreed
with the solid on hole pitch (27.70 vs 26.82), flange width (31.80 vs 33.00) and
shaft offset (0.65 vs 5.42). **The solid was right every time** — confirmed on
hardware when the servo mounted securely on the 26.82 pitch.

**Fits one way up only: the closer flange hole is the LOWER one.** Upside down

### Orientation decision, so it is not re-litigated

Vertical was the builder's call and it was right. I pushed back twice claiming it
cost 6.25 mm of wing offset; **the real cost is 1.75 mm**, and my figure was
inflated by the transcribed shaft offset. Standing the servo up also moved the
servo's asymmetry onto Z, where it is a fixed offset — both hands are now
identical apart from a mirror (bolt-to-wall 8.20 either way), instead of
3.15/4.45 swapping sides as they did horizontally.

The horizontal generator is preserved at `cad/attic/`.

### Deck hole mismatch — RESOLVED, was a measurement reference

Reported twice as "1–2 mm too far out". The meshes agreed to 0.03 mm throughout.
The builder's 6.55 mm pin-to-bolt reading was taken from the pin's **edge**;
6.55 + 1.995 (pin radius) = 8.55 against a nominal 8.50. Nothing was wrong. The
bolt slots added to absorb it are harmless and stay.

**Unambiguous checks for next time** — both features are holes, no centre-finding:
bolt-to-bolt inside-to-inside **35.60**; pin-to-pin outside-to-outside **25.99**.

## The pattern worth remembering

The builder caught **every one of the last dozen faults** — from slicer previews,
from renders, and by asking "how is this supposed to work?". My per-part checks
kept passing parts that could not be assembled. What finally changed the outcome
was not a better checklist but a different kind of check: **boolean the part
against a solid model of what it holds.** See `feedback-cad-audit-checklist` in
memory, step 0.
